# EdgeDepth — Failure analysis

All experiments are reproducible with `python scripts/failure_cases.py` (results in
`results/failure_cases.json`, figures in `results/figures/failure_*.png`) and can be run live in the app's
**Failure Analysis** section. They use 8 unseen NYU test frames (every third test frame).

Method set: bicubic, JBU, guided filter, EdgeDepthNet, hybrid. Five genuine failure modes were measured;
numbers are means over the 8 frames (tables regenerated by `scripts/make_report.py`).

## F1 — Extremely sparse depth input

**Setup.** Random sparse samples at densities from 10 % down to 0.2 % of the 320×240 pixels.

<!-- AUTO:failure_F1 -->
RMSE (m) vs sparse density

| Method | 10 % | 5 % | 2 % | 1 % | 0.5 % | 0.2 % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bicubic | 0.0727 | 0.0967 | 0.1392 | 0.1738 | 0.2252 | 0.2747 |
| Joint bilateral (JBU) | 0.0894 | 0.1116 | 0.1472 | 0.1786 | 0.2335 | 0.2958 |
| Guided filter | 0.0714 | 0.0946 | 0.1293 | 0.1650 | 0.2120 | 0.2625 |
| Deep learning (EdgeDepthNet) | 0.0645 | 0.0826 | 0.1112 | 0.1304 | 0.1699 | 0.2325 |
| Hybrid (fusion + edge-aware refinement) | 0.0561 | 0.0753 | 0.1034 | 0.1223 | 0.1614 | 0.2264 |
<!-- /AUTO:failure_F1 -->

**What fails.** Error roughly quadruples from 10 % to 0.2 % density for every method. At 0.2 % the mean spacing between
samples is ≈ 22 px, so whole objects (chair legs, shelf boards, small boxes) contain *no* measurement; interpolators
produce Delaunay/Voronoi facets, JBU spreads the few samples along colour regions, and the network and hybrid fill in
plausible but wrong surfaces from RGB context (figure `results/figures/failure_F1_sparse_0.2pct.png`). The hybrid stays
best at every density, but at ≤ 0.5 % its RMSE (> 0.16 m) is no longer useful for geometry.

**Why.** Reconstruction can only be as good as the evidence; below ~1 % the problem becomes monocular depth
estimation, which a 0.7 M-parameter network trained on 126 frames cannot solve. The network also saw densities only
down to 1 % during training.

**Improvements.** Train with densities down to 0.1 %; add a monocular depth prior (e.g. a distilled MiDaS/Depth
Anything) as an additional expert; output an uncertainty map so the UI can flag unsupported regions.

## F2 — Large missing region

**Setup.** At 4×, a rectangle covering ~20 % of the low-res map (a central region including object boundaries) is
removed. RMSE is measured inside and outside the removed region.

<!-- AUTO:failure_F2 -->
| Method | RMSE inside hole (m) | RMSE outside hole (m) |
|---|---|---|
| Bicubic | 0.4777 | 0.0580 |
| Joint bilateral (JBU) | 0.4703 | 0.0747 |
| Guided filter | 0.4770 | 0.0682 |
| Deep learning (EdgeDepthNet) | 0.4391 | 0.0446 |
| Hybrid (fusion + edge-aware refinement) | 0.4388 | 0.0413 |
<!-- /AUTO:failure_F2 -->

**What fails.** Inside the hole every method is ≈ 10× worse than outside (0.44–0.48 m vs 0.04–0.07 m). Push-pull,
bicubic and the RGB-guided filters stretch a smooth membrane from the hole border; the network uses RGB context and is
8 % better inside the hole, but objects that lie entirely inside the hole keep a wrong absolute depth
(`results/figures/failure_F2_large_hole.png`). The back-projection cannot help there because it only uses measured
pixels (M = 0 in the hole) — by design it never invents corrections.

**Why.** No measurement constrains the absolute depth inside the hole; RGB gives shape cues but not metric depth.

**Improvements.** Train with larger synthetic holes (the training blobs were ≤ 1/7 of the image); propagate depth along
planar surfaces detected in RGB (plane fitting / Manhattan priors); flag large holes as low confidence.

## F3 — Excessive noise

**Setup.** 4× input with the Kinect noise model scaled ×1, ×2, ×4, ×8 (σ at 4 m ≈ 2.6 cm per unit).

<!-- AUTO:failure_F3 -->
RMSE (m) at 4×

| Method | noise ×1 | noise ×2 | noise ×4 | noise ×8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bicubic | 0.0619 | 0.0656 | 0.0783 | 0.1151 |
| Joint bilateral (JBU) | 0.0765 | 0.0780 | 0.0836 | 0.1025 |
| Guided filter | 0.0712 | 0.0729 | 0.0792 | 0.0998 |
| Deep learning (EdgeDepthNet) | 0.0470 | 0.0500 | 0.0628 | 0.1072 |
| Hybrid (fusion + edge-aware refinement) | 0.0451 | 0.0501 | 0.0627 | 0.1068 |
<!-- /AUTO:failure_F3 -->

**What fails.** Up to ×2 the network and hybrid are clearly best. At ×4 their advantage shrinks and at ×8 they are
*worse than the guided filter and JBU* (0.107 m vs 0.100 / 0.103 m), see `results/figures/failure_F3_noise_x8.png`.
During development the un-gated back-projection made this much worse (hybrid 0.135 m at ×8 because it re-injected
the noisy measurements); the noise-adaptive gain α(q), fitted on validation, fixed that regression, so the hybrid now
tracks the network.

**Why.** Training noise covered ×0.3–×2.5; at ×8 the per-sample error exceeds many real depth steps, so the network
(trained to trust its input) passes noise and false edges through. The guided filter's local linear model averages
over larger windows and is more robust to this out-of-distribution noise. At 8× the noise estimator also confuses
scene structure with noise (§13 of the design document).

**Improvements.** Wider noise range and noise-level conditioning during training; feed the estimated σ to the
network; use the guided filter's output as the D0 input when the noise estimate is high.

## F4 — Texture-copying artefacts

**Setup.** Ground truth is a smooth slanted plane (2–4 m); the RGB guide is a tiled, strongly textured test image, so
every RGB edge is a *texture* edge. 8× input, no noise. Texture-copy ratio = mean error on (dilated) RGB Canny edges
divided by the mean error elsewhere; 1 means the error is unrelated to texture.

<!-- AUTO:failure_F4 -->
| Method | RMSE (mm) | MAE on RGB texture edges (mm) | MAE elsewhere (mm) | Texture-copy ratio (1 = none) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bicubic | 2.69 | 1.81 | 2.08 | 0.87 |
| Joint bilateral (JBU) | 7.49 | 6.17 | 3.72 | 1.66 |
| Guided filter | 3.69 | 2.29 | 2.40 | 0.96 |
| Deep learning (EdgeDepthNet) | 8.77 | 6.44 | 5.84 | 1.10 |
| Hybrid (fusion + edge-aware refinement) | 5.36 | 3.53 | 2.67 | 1.32 |
<!-- /AUTO:failure_F4 -->

**What fails.** JBU copies texture the most (ratio 1.66): its range kernel splits a flat region into colour segments
with slightly different depths. The hybrid's error is 1.32× higher on texture edges — the RGB term of its
back-projection carries some texture into the correction. The network's depth gate keeps its ratio near 1 (1.10), but
on this out-of-distribution scene (no real objects) the network's absolute error is the largest (8.8 mm), and bicubic,
which ignores RGB, is best (2.7 mm). All errors are millimetres here; on real scenes the same mechanism produces
visible ripples on walls with posters or patterned floors (`results/figures/failure_F4_texture_copy.png`).

**Why.** Any method that lets colour similarity steer depth assumes "colour edge ⇒ depth edge"; texture violates it.

**Improvements.** Gate the RGB term of the back-projection by the network's boundary probability (use RGB only where
B_dl is high); add synthetic planar-texture samples to training; increase the guided-filter ε in regions the texture
cue flags.

## F5 — Incorrect boundaries from RGB–depth misalignment

**Setup.** At 8×, the RGB image is shifted horizontally by 0, 2, 4, 8 px relative to the depth (a calibration or
synchronisation error).

<!-- AUTO:failure_F5 -->
Boundary RMSE (m) / Edge F1 at 8×

| Method | shift 0 px | shift 2 px | shift 4 px | shift 8 px |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bicubic | 0.2451 / 0.314 | 0.2451 / 0.314 | 0.2451 / 0.314 | 0.2451 / 0.314 |
| Joint bilateral (JBU) | 0.2802 / 0.645 | 0.2709 / 0.670 | 0.2857 / 0.578 | 0.2924 / 0.476 |
| Guided filter | 0.2691 / 0.612 | 0.2678 / 0.611 | 0.2723 / 0.599 | 0.2745 / 0.581 |
| Deep learning (EdgeDepthNet) | 0.1987 / 0.779 | 0.1986 / 0.782 | 0.2276 / 0.739 | 0.2336 / 0.685 |
| Hybrid (fusion + edge-aware refinement) | 0.1879 / 0.790 | 0.1877 / 0.793 | 0.2108 / 0.737 | 0.2211 / 0.706 |
<!-- /AUTO:failure_F5 -->

**What fails.** Bicubic is unaffected (it ignores RGB). Every RGB-guided method degrades: at 8 px the hybrid's boundary
RMSE rises from 0.188 to 0.221 m and edge F1 from 0.79 to 0.71; the network loses 0.09 F1. Edges are reconstructed
where the *colour* edge is, i.e. in the wrong place (`results/figures/failure_F5_misaligned_rgb.png`). Even so, the
hybrid at 8 px misalignment is still better than bicubic on boundary RMSE (0.221 vs 0.245 m).

**Why.** Guidance assumes pixel-accurate registration; a shifted guide moves the predicted discontinuities.

**Improvements.** Estimate and compensate the misalignment (e.g. maximise the correlation between depth and RGB
gradients over small shifts/flows) before guidance; train with small random misalignments for robustness.

## Summary

| Failure | Severity for the hybrid | Root cause | Most promising fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 extreme sparsity | high below 1 % | no evidence; outside training range | monocular prior expert, training at 0.1 % |
| F2 large holes | high inside the hole | no metric constraint | larger training holes, planar propagation |
| F3 excessive noise | moderate (≥ ×4) | out-of-distribution noise | noise conditioning, wider training range |
| F4 texture copying | low (mm), but visible | RGB term in back-projection | gate RGB term by boundary probability |
| F5 misalignment | moderate at ≥ 4 px | guidance assumes registration | alignment estimation before guidance |
